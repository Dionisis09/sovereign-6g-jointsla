# Introductory paragraph

Future 6G services increasingly connect user-facing outcomes to latency, priority and resource requirements spanning a heterogeneous telco cloud continuum. Within the SOVEREIGN-6G architectural context, a Digital Service Orchestrator provides a natural setting for coordinating compute, network and GPU resources in support of SLA assurance [1]. We position JointSLA as a candidate decision function beneath this orchestration layer, consuming structured, intent-derived requirements after their translation by an upstream management function. The study does not implement intent interpretation or a complete SOVEREIGN platform. Instead, it examines the downstream systems problem: under which operating conditions does coupled placement, scaling and queue-aware request routing improve edge-AI performance, and when do consolidation, limited warm capacity and cold-start recovery reverse those benefits? Through paired evaluation and hardware-grounded characterization, the completed experiments expose a regime-dependent performance/resource/recovery trade-off. A frozen held-out mitigation experiment further shows that protecting Gold traffic improves its SLA outcomes relative to JointSLA-v1 without eliminating the underlying trade-off. The contribution therefore concerns the operating envelope and resource interactions of coupled orchestration, while treating intent as the upstream source of technical constraints rather than an additional novelty claim.

[1] [Iquadrat SOVEREIGN-6G project overview](https://www.iquadrat.com/rd/sovereign-6g/), accessed 7 October 2026. The DSO placement of JointSLA is proposed conceptual integration. See [field mapping and scope boundaries](SOVEREIGN_INTENT_MAPPING.md).
